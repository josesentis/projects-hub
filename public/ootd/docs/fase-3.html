OOTD — Onboarding journey (capturing taste, references & wardrobe)

A brief for Claude Code. Companion to OOTD_app_journey_brief.md. Goal: get the user from install → their first outfit with as little friction as possible, while gathering the data the stylist needs (taste, references, wardrobe) progressively rather than in one giant form.

The core tension

The styling engine wants a lot: weighted vibes, fit/modesty prefs, reference folders, and ~40 wardrobe items with photos. Asking for all of that up front = a wall of forms = churn before the first "wow".

Philosophy: deliver value fast, earn the data over time.

  • Get to a first real outfit in under ~3 minutes, even with a thin wardrobe.
  • Make every input method fast, visual and a little fun (swipes, camera, import) — never a spreadsheet.
  • Be honest about confidence ("I've got the gist — add a few more pieces and I'll really nail it") so the user wants to give more.
  • Enrich forever, not just at onboarding — the app keeps learning as you wear, save and react.

The onboarding flow (progressive)

Step 0 — Welcome (1 screen, ~5s). The promise, warm and short: "I'm your stylist. Tell me what you like, show me your closet, and I'll dress you every morning." One Get started.

Step 1 — Coarse taste seed (visual, ~20s). "Which of these feel like you?" — a grid of aesthetic cards (Workwear, Quiet Luxury, Streetwear, Minimal, Old Money, Y2K, Sporty, Bohemian…), pick 2-4. This seeds Layer-1 identity instantly with zero typing. (Multi-select, no wrong answers.)

Step 2 — Taste refinement game (swipe, ~45s, fun). A "this or you?" swipe deck: ~12-15 outfit images, swipe ♥ / ✕ (or "this vs that" pairs). This builds the taste vector far more accurately than self-reported labels — and feels like a game, not a form. Show a tiny live read ("getting a feel for you… 🔥"). Skippable after a few.

Step 3 — Quick practical prefs (taps, ~20s). Only the few that change outputs: fit (loose / fitted / mix), colours you avoid (tap swatches), modesty / coverage if relevant, no-go items ("never heels"). Three taps, not a questionnaire. Everything optional with sensible defaults.

Step 4 — Wardrobe seed (the hard one — make it effortless). Don't ask for 40 items. Ask for a "core 10" — your most-worn pieces — to get going. Offer several capture methods, pick whichever the user likes:

  • 📸 Closet scan (hero method): "Lay some pieces out or open your wardrobe and snap a photo — I'll detect the items." Use multi-garment detection so one photo = several items auto-cropped & categorised; user confirms/edits in a quick review grid. Far faster than one-by-one.
  • 🧺 One-tap from photos: scan the camera roll for clothing flat-lays/outfit pics already there.
  • 🛍️ Import (future): from order-history emails / retailer accounts.
  • ✋ Manual add: snap or pick one item, auto-fill category/colour, user tweaks. Always available as fallback. Show a progress ring ("7 of your core 10") and let them finish anytime — the wardrobe is never "done", it grows.

Step 5 — References (optional, skippable, ~15s). "Got a place you save looks?" — connect/import: Pinterest board, Instagram saved, or drop screenshots (most people have a screenshots folder). Set up the share-sheet here too so saving to OOTD from anywhere becomes a habit. Fully skippable — can be added later.

Step 6 — First outfit reveal (the payoff). 🎉 Immediately show a real look from their seeded wardrobe + taste, framed in the assistant's voice, with the honest confidence note: "Here's your first look — I'm ~70% sure of your taste. The more you wear and save, the sharper I get." This is the moment that earns continued investment.

Step 7 — Set the habit. Ask permission for morning notifications ("Want me to have a look ready each morning?") + location/weather access (explained in plain terms: "so I can dress you for the actual weather"). Done.

Ongoing enrichment (after onboarding — just as important)

The profile keeps sharpening through normal use, never another form:

  • "Mark worn" on accepted looks → wear history (no-repeat + favourites).
  • Accept / reject / swap every day → continuously refines the taste vector.
  • Share-sheet saves → reference folders grow passively.
  • "Add as you go": when a look references something not owned, or the user wears something new, a gentle one-tap "add this to your wardrobe?".
  • Seasonal nudges: "Add a few warm pieces? Winter's coming and your closet's light on knitwear." Frames a data gap as helpful, not a chore.
  • Confidence meter somewhere subtle, so the user sees giving more = better results.

Principles checklist (for the design)

  • ⏱️ First outfit < 3 min, even with ~10 items.
  • 🎈 Every step skippable / finishable — never a hard gate except the absolute minimum (a coarse taste seed + a handful of items).
  • 👆 Taps & swipes & camera over typing. Typing is the last resort.
  • 📈 Progressive disclosure — coarse → fine; seed now, enrich forever.
  • 🫶 Warm, honest copy — confidence over false precision; gaps framed as "I'll get better".
  • 🔁 Onboarding never really ends — the best data comes from daily use, so design for that.
